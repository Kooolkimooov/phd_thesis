\section{Robot chain modelling}
\label{rcc_robot_chain_modelling}

The inertial frame, denoted as \lframe{I} when needed, is an earth-fixed, right-handed frame.
Throughout this work we adopt a local \ac{enu} convention unless otherwise stated: the
\vect{x}-axis points to the east, the \vect{y}-axis points to the north and the \vect{z}-axis
points up. The origin of this frame is located at the water surface (\cref{rcc_rcm_frames}).

The body-fixed frame, denoted as \lframe{B} when needed, is rigidly attached to the vehicle with
origin at the vehicle centre of gravity. It is a right-handed frame with the \vect{x}-axis pointing
forward, the \vect{y}-axis pointing to the left and the \vect{z}-axis pointing up
(\cref{rcc_rcm_frames}).

\subsection{Single robot dynamics}
\label{rcc_rcm_single_robot_dynamics}

The modelling of a \acp{rov} is described by the classical six-degree-of-freedom rigid body model
formulated for marine craft, as detailed in~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}:
\begin{align}
	\vect{\dot{\eta}} & = \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \vect{\nu} \label{rcc_rcm_srd_kinematics} \\
	\lrdmatr{M}{rb} \vect{\dot{\nu}} + \lrdmatr{M}{a} \ldvect{\dot{\nu}}{r}
	                  & + \lrdmatr{C}{rb}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \vect{\nu}
	+ \left(\lrdmatr{C}{a}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right) + \matr{D}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right)\right) \ldvect{\nu}{r}
	+ \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) = \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_dynamics}
\end{align}

In these equations:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\vect{\eta} = \left[\scal{x}, \scal{y}, \scal{z}, \scal{\phi}, \scal{\theta}, \scal{\psi}\right]^{\top}$ denotes the vehicle's position and orientation in the inertial frame \lframe{I}.
	\item $\vect{\nu} = \left[\scal{u}, \scal{v}, \scal{w}, \scal{p}, \scal{q}, \scal{r}\right]^{\top}$ is the linear and angular velocity in the body-fixed frame \lframe{B}.
	\item $\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ is the transformation matrix mapping body frame to inertial frame.
	\item \ldvect{\nu}{r} represents the relative velocity between the vehicle and the surrounding water, accounting for current disturbances.
	\item \lrdmatr{M}{rb} and \lrdmatr{M}{a} are the rigid-body and added mass inertia matrices, respectively.
	\item \lrdmatr{C}{rb} and \lrdmatr{C}{a} are their associated Coriolis matrices.
	\item \matr{D} is the hydrodynamic damping matrix.
	\item $\vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ represents the hydrostatic restoring force.
	\item \vect{\omega} includes environmental disturbances such as wind and wave loads.
	\item \vect{\tau} is the control input.
\end{itemize}

Under the assumptions of irrotational underwater currents and operation without dynamic ballast
systems, the dynamic model simplifies to the following form~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}:
\begin{align}
	\matr{I} \vect{\dot{\nu}}
	+ \left( \matr{C}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) + \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \right) \vect{\nu}
	+ \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)
	= \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_dynamics_simple}
\end{align}

Here, \matr{I} is the generalized inertia matrix, \matr{C} is the generalized Coriolis matrix, and
\matr{D} is the generalized damping matrix.

Furthermore, \cref{sota_sm_um_kinematics,sota_sm_um_dynamics_simple} may be combined into a
state-space representation:
\begin{align}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\lrdmatr{I}{d} & 0        \\
		0              & \matr{I}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\vect{\dot{\eta}} \\
		\vect{\dot{\nu}}
	\end{bmatrix} & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 & \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)                                   \\
		0 & -\matr{C}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) - \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\vect{\eta} \\
		\vect{\nu}
	\end{bmatrix}
	+
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 \\
		\vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau} - \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

With \lrdmatr{I}{d} the identity matrix.

The transformation matrix $\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ introduced in
\cref{rcc_rcm_srd_kinematics} can be partitioned as
\begin{align}
	\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\phi,\theta,\psi\right) & \lrdmatr{0}{3\times 3}                 \\
		\lrdmatr{0}{3\times 3}                      & \lrdmatr{R}{a}\left(\phi,\theta\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta},\scal{\psi}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ is the rotation matrix mapping linear
	      velocities from the body-fixed frame to the inertial ENU frame.
	\item $\lrdmatr{R}{a}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ is the transformation relating Euler
	      angle rates to angular velocities in the body-fixed frame.
\end{itemize}

The rotation matrix \lrdmatr{R}{l} is obtained from the composition of three elementary rotations,
following the ZYX Euler angle convention (yaw-pitch-roll). In this convention, the orientation of
the body relative to the inertial frame is parameterised by the angles:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \scal{\psi}: yaw, a rotation about the inertial \fvect{z}{I}-axis,
	\item \scal{\theta}: pitch, a rotation about the intermediate \fluvect{y}{I}{'}-axis (after the yaw rotation),
	\item \scal{\phi}: roll, a rotation about the body-fixed \fvect{x}{B}-axis (after the yaw and pitch rotations).
\end{itemize}

Each of these rotations is represented by a basic rotation matrix. \trigsh{}
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{x}}\left(\scal{\phi}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0                 & 0                  \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}} & -\sin{\scal{\phi}} \\
		0 & \sin{\scal{\phi}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}
	\end{bmatrix} &
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{y}}\left(\scal{\theta}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\cos{\scal{\theta}}  & 0 & \sin{\scal{\theta}} \\
		0                    & 1 & 0                   \\
		-\sin{\scal{\theta}} & 0 & \cos{\scal{\theta}}
	\end{bmatrix} &
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{z}}\left(\scal{\psi}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\cos{\scal{\psi}} & -\sin{\scal{\psi}} & 0 \\
		\sin{\scal{\psi}} & \cos{\scal{\psi}}  & 0 \\
		0                 & 0                  & 1
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

This composition outlined above then yields
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta},\scal{\psi}\right) & = \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{z}}\left(\scal{\psi}\right) \times \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{y}}\left(\scal{\theta}\right) \times \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{x}}\left(\scal{\phi}\right) \\
	                                                                 & = \begin{bmatrix}
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\theta}}                                                         &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \sin{\scal{\phi}} - \sin{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} + \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\phi}}   \\
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\theta}}                                                         &
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \sin{\scal{\phi}} + \cos{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} &
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} - \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\phi}}   \\
		                                                                     -\sin{\scal{\theta}}                                                                          &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\theta}}\sin{\scal{\phi}}                                                          &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\theta}}\cos{\scal{\phi}}
	                                                                     \end{bmatrix}
	\label{eq:rov_rotation_matrix_final}
\end{align}

The transformation \lrdmatr{R}{a} between body angular velocities and Euler angle rates can be
derived systematically from the ZYX Euler parameterization. The instantaneous angular velocity
vector can be expressed as a linear combination of the contributions from the Euler angle rates:
\begin{align}
	\vect{\omega} & = \dot{\phi}\fvect{x}{B} + \dot{\theta}\fluvect{y}{I}{'} + \dot{\psi}\fvect{z}{I}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_angle_composition}
\end{align}

Since we require the angular velocity expressed in the body-fixed frame, we project each of these
axes into \lframe{B}:

\begin{align}
	\fvect{x}{B} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\0 \\0\end{bmatrix}                                                                                                    &
	\fvect{y}{B} = \lrmatr{R}{\top}{x}\left(\phi\right) \begin{bmatrix}0 \\1 \\0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \cos{\phi} \\ \sin{\phi} \end{bmatrix} &
	\fvect{z}{B} = {\left(\lrdmatr{R}{y}\left(\theta\right)\lrdmatr{R}{x}\left(\phi\right)\right)}^{\top} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-\sin{\theta} \\ \cos{\theta} \sin{\phi} \\ \cos{\theta} \cos{\phi} \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

Substituting these into \cref{rcc_rcm_srd_angle_composition} and expressing in the body frame:
\begin{align}
	\fvect{\omega}{B} & =
	\dot{\phi}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
	+ \dot{\theta}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \cos\phi \\ \sin\phi \end{bmatrix}
	+ \dot{\psi}\begin{bmatrix} -\sin\theta \\ \cos\theta\sin\phi \\ \cos\theta\cos\phi \end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}

Collecting terms, we obtain the relation
\begin{align}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{p} \\ \scal{q} \\ \scal{r}
	\end{bmatrix} & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0                  & -\sin{\scal{\theta}}                 \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}}  & \cos{\scal{\theta}}\sin{\scal{\phi}} \\
		0 & -\sin{\scal{\phi}} & \cos{\scal{\theta}}\cos{\scal{\phi}}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{\dot{\phi}} \\ \scal{\dot{\theta}} \\ \scal{\dot{\psi}}
	\end{bmatrix} =
	\lrmatr{R}{-1}{a}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{\dot{\phi}} \\ \scal{\dot{\theta}} \\ \scal{\dot{\psi}}
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\lrdmatr{R}{a}                   & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & \sin{\scal{\phi}}\tan{\scal{\theta}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}\tan{\scal{\theta}} \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}}                    & -\sin{\scal{\phi}}                   \\
		0 & \sin{\scal{\phi}}\cos{\scal{\theta}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}\cos{\scal{\theta}}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

which becomes singular at $\scal{\theta} = \pm{}\qty{90}{\degree}$ (gimbal lock).
