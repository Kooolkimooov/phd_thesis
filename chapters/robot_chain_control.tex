\section{Robot chain modelling}
\label{rcc_robot_chain_modelling}

The inertial frame, denoted as \lframe{I} when required, was defined as an earth-fixed,
right-handed frame. A local \ac{enu} convention was adopted unless explicitly stated otherwise: the
\vect{x}-axis pointed east, the \vect{y}-axis pointed north, and the \vect{z}-axis pointed upward.
The origin of this frame was located at the water surface (\cref{rcc_rcm_frames}).

The body-fixed frame, denoted as \lframe{B} when required, was rigidly attached to the vehicle with
its origin at the centre of gravity. It was a right-handed frame in which the \vect{x}-axis pointed
forward, the \vect{y}-axis pointed to the left, and the \vect{z}-axis pointed upward
(\cref{rcc_rcm_frames}).

\subsection{Single robot dynamics}
\label{rcc_rcm_single_robot_dynamics}

The dynamics of a \ac{rov} are described by the classical six-degree-of-freedom rigid-body model
formulated for marine craft~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}:
\begin{align}
	\vect{\dot{\eta}} & = \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) \vect{\nu} \label{rcc_rcm_srd_kinematics} \\
	\lrdmatr{M}{rb} \vect{\dot{\nu}} + \lrdmatr{M}{a} \ldvect{\dot{\nu}}{r}
	                  & + \lrdmatr{C}{rb}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \vect{\nu}
	+ \left(\lrdmatr{C}{a}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right) + \matr{D}\left(\ldvect{\nu}{r}\right)\right) \ldvect{\nu}{r}
	+ \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) = \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_dynamics}
\end{align}

In these equations:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\vect{\eta} = \left[\scal{x}, \scal{y}, \scal{z}, \scal{\phi}, \scal{\theta}, \scal{\psi}\right]^{\top}$ denotes the vehicle position and orientation in the inertial frame \lframe{I}.
	\item $\vect{\nu} = \left[\scal{u}, \scal{v}, \scal{w}, \scal{p}, \scal{q}, \scal{r}\right]^{\top}$ is the linear and angular velocity in the body-fixed frame \lframe{B}.
	\item $\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ is the transformation matrix mapping the body-fixed frame to the inertial frame.
	\item \ldvect{\nu}{r} represents the relative velocity between the vehicle and the surrounding water, accounting for current disturbances.
	\item \lrdmatr{M}{rb} and \lrdmatr{M}{a} are the rigid-body and added-mass inertia matrices, respectively.
	\item \lrdmatr{C}{rb} and \lrdmatr{C}{a} are the associated Coriolis matrices.
	\item \matr{D} is the hydrodynamic damping matrix.
	\item $\vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ represents the hydrostatic restoring force.
	\item \vect{\omega} accounts for environmental disturbances such as wind and wave loads.
	\item \vect{\tau} is the control input.
\end{itemize}

Under the assumptions of irrotational underwater currents, operation without dynamic ballast
systems, and negligible Coriolis effects at the low to moderate speeds considered, the dynamic
model was simplified to~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}:
\begin{align}
	\matr{I} \vect{\dot{\nu}}
	+ \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) \vect{\nu}
	+ \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)
	= \vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_dynamics_simple}
\end{align}

Here, \matr{I} is the generalized inertia matrix and \matr{D} is the generalized damping matrix.

Furthermore, \cref{rcc_rcm_srd_kinematics,rcc_rcm_srd_dynamics_simple} can be combined into a
state-space representation:
\begin{align}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\lrdmatr{I}{6\times 6} & 0        \\
		0                      & \matr{I}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\vect{\dot{\eta}} \\
		\vect{\dot{\nu}}
	\end{bmatrix} & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 & \matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)  \\
		0 & - \matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\vect{\eta} \\
		\vect{\nu}
	\end{bmatrix}
	+
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 \\
		\vect{\omega} + \vect{\tau} - \vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

The transformation matrix $\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right)$ introduced in
\cref{rcc_rcm_srd_kinematics} is partitioned as
\begin{align}
	\matr{T}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\phi,\theta,\psi\right) & \lrdmatr{0}{3\times 3}                 \\
		\lrdmatr{0}{3\times 3}                      & \lrdmatr{R}{a}\left(\phi,\theta\right)
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

where:
\begin{itemize}
	\item $\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta},\scal{\psi}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ is the rotation matrix mapping linear velocities from the body-fixed frame to the inertial ENU frame.
	\item $\lrdmatr{R}{a}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$ is the transformation relating Euler angle rates to angular velocities in the body-fixed frame.
\end{itemize}

The rotation matrix \lrdmatr{R}{l} is obtained by composing three elementary rotations following
the ZYX Euler angle convention (yaw-pitch-roll). In this convention, the orientation of the body
relative to the inertial frame is parameterised by:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \scal{\psi}: yaw, a rotation about the inertial \fvect{z}{I}-axis,
	\item \scal{\theta}: pitch, a rotation about the intermediate \fluvect{y}{I}{'}-axis (after the yaw rotation),
	\item \scal{\phi}: roll, a rotation about the body-fixed \fvect{x}{B}-axis (after the yaw and pitch rotations).
\end{itemize}

Each of these rotations is represented by a basic rotation matrix. \trigsh{}
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{x}}\left(\scal{\phi}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0                 & 0                  \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}} & -\sin{\scal{\phi}} \\
		0 & \sin{\scal{\phi}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}
	\end{bmatrix} \hspace{.5cm}
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{y}}\left(\scal{\theta}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\cos{\scal{\theta}}  & 0 & \sin{\scal{\theta}} \\
		0                    & 1 & 0                   \\
		-\sin{\scal{\theta}} & 0 & \cos{\scal{\theta}}
	\end{bmatrix} \hspace{.5cm}
	\lrdmatr{R}{\vect{z}}\left(\scal{\psi}\right) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\cos{\scal{\psi}} & -\sin{\scal{\psi}} & 0 \\
		\sin{\scal{\psi}} & \cos{\scal{\psi}}  & 0 \\
		0                 & 0                  & 1
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

The composition yields
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{R}{l}\left(\scal{\phi},\scal{\theta},\scal{\psi}\right) & = \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{z}}\left(\scal{\psi}\right) \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{y}}\left(\scal{\theta}\right) \lrdmatr{R}{\vect{x}}\left(\scal{\phi}\right)               \\
	                                                                 & = \begin{bmatrix}
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\theta}}                                                         &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \sin{\scal{\phi}} - \sin{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} + \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\phi}}   \\
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\theta}}                                                         &
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \sin{\scal{\phi}} + \cos{\scal{\psi}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} &
		                                                                     \sin{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\theta}} \cos{\scal{\phi}} - \cos{\scal{\psi}} \sin{\scal{\phi}}   \\
		                                                                     -\sin{\scal{\theta}}                                                                          &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\theta}}\sin{\scal{\phi}}                                                          &
		                                                                     \cos{\scal{\theta}}\cos{\scal{\phi}}
	                                                                     \end{bmatrix}
	\label{eq:rov_rotation_matrix_final}
\end{align}

The transformation \lrdmatr{R}{a} between body angular velocities and Euler angle rates is derived
systematically from the ZYX Euler parameterization. The instantaneous angular velocity vector is
expressed as a linear combination of contributions from the Euler angle rates:
\begin{align}
	\vect{\omega} & = \dot{\phi}\fvect{x}{B} + \dot{\theta}\fluvect{y}{I}{'} + \dot{\psi}\fvect{z}{I}
	\label{rcc_rcm_srd_angle_composition}
\end{align}

Since the angular velocity must be expressed in the body-fixed frame, each axis is projected into
\lframe{B}:
\begin{align}
	\fvect{x}{B} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\0 \\0\end{bmatrix}                                                                                                    &
	\fvect{y}{B} = \lrmatr{R}{\top}{x}\left(\phi\right) \begin{bmatrix}0 \\1 \\0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \cos{\phi} \\ \sin{\phi} \end{bmatrix} &
	\fvect{z}{B} = {\left(\lrdmatr{R}{y}\left(\theta\right)\lrdmatr{R}{x}\left(\phi\right)\right)}^{\top} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-\sin{\theta} \\ \cos{\theta} \sin{\phi} \\ \cos{\theta} \cos{\phi} \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

Substituting these into \cref{rcc_rcm_srd_angle_composition} and expressing in the body frame:
\begin{align}
	\fvect{\omega}{B} & =
	\dot{\phi}\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
	+ \dot{\theta}\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \cos{\scal{\phi}} \\ \sin{\scal{\phi}} \end{bmatrix}
	+ \dot{\psi}\begin{bmatrix} -\sin{\scal{\theta}} \\ \cos{\scal{\theta}}\sin{\scal{\phi}} \\ \cos{\scal{\theta}}\cos{\scal{\phi}} \end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}

By collecting terms, the relation is obtained:
\begin{align}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{p} \\ \scal{q} \\ \scal{r}
	\end{bmatrix} & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 0                  & -\sin{\scal{\theta}}                 \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}}  & \cos{\scal{\theta}}\sin{\scal{\phi}} \\
		0 & -\sin{\scal{\phi}} & \cos{\scal{\theta}}\cos{\scal{\phi}}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{\dot{\phi}} \\ \scal{\dot{\theta}} \\ \scal{\dot{\psi}}
	\end{bmatrix} =
	\lrmatr{R}{-1}{a}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\scal{\dot{\phi}} \\ \scal{\dot{\theta}} \\ \scal{\dot{\psi}}
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\lrdmatr{R}{a}                   & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & \sin{\scal{\phi}}\tan{\scal{\theta}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}\tan{\scal{\theta}} \\
		0 & \cos{\scal{\phi}}                    & -\sin{\scal{\phi}}                   \\
		0 & \sin{\scal{\phi}}\cos{\scal{\theta}} & \cos{\scal{\phi}}\cos{\scal{\theta}}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

This expression becomes singular at $\scal{\theta} = \pm{}\qty{90}{\degree}$ (gimbal lock).

\trigft{}

The hydrostatic restoring vector is given by
\begin{align}
	\vect{s}\left(\vect{\eta}\right) & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\fldvect{f}{B}{g} + \fldvect{f}{B}{b} \\
		\fldvect{m}{B}{g} + \fldvect{m}{B}{b}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

where the first three components are the net forces due to gravity and buoyancy, and the last three
components are the corresponding restoring moments.

In the inertial frame \lframe{I}, the gravitational and buoyancy forces are
\begin{align}
	\fldvect{f}{I}{g} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 \\ 0 \\ -\scal{m}\scal{g}
	\end{bmatrix} \hspace{1cm}
	\fldvect{f}{I}{b} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		0 \\ 0 \\ \ldscal{\rho}{w}\scal{g}\scal{v}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

where \scal{m} is the vehicle mass, \scal{g} is the gravitational acceleration, \ldscal{\rho}{w} is
the water density, and \scal{v} is the submerged volume of the vehicle.

The rotation matrix \lrdmatr{R}{l} is used to express these forces in \lframe{B}
\begin{align}
	\fldvect{f}{B}{g} = \lrdmatr{R}{l}^{\top}(\phi,\theta,\psi) \fldvect{f}{I}{g} \\
	\fldvect{f}{B}{b} = \lrdmatr{R}{l}^{\top}(\phi,\theta,\psi) \fldvect{f}{I}{b}
\end{align}

The corresponding restoring moments are obtained from the moment arms of the centre of gravity
$\ldvect{c}{g}=\left[\ldscal{c}{g,x}, \ldscal{c}{g,y}, \ldscal{c}{g,z}\right]^{\top}$ and centre of
buoyancy $\ldvect{c}{b}=\left[\ldscal{c}{b,x}, \ldscal{c}{b,y}, \ldscal{c}{b,z}\right]^{\top}$
\begin{align}
	\ldvect{m}{g} & = \ldvect{r}{g} \times \left( \lrmatr{R}{\top}{l}\left(\phi,\theta,\psi\right)\fldvect{f}{I}{g} \right) \\
	\ldvect{m}{b} & = \ldvect{r}{b} \times \left( \lrmatr{R}{\top}{l}\left(\phi,\theta,\psi\right)\fldvect{f}{I}{b} \right)
\end{align}

The generalized inertia matrix \matr{I} represents the combined rigid-body and added-mass effects:
\begin{align}
	\matr{I} = \lrdmatr{M}{rb} + \lrdmatr{M}{a}.
\end{align}

The rigid-body inertia matrix \lrdmatr{M}{rb} is expressed as
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{M}{rb} & =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		m \lrdmatr{I}{3\times 3}             & -m \matr{S}\left(\ldvect{c}{g}\right) \\
		m \matr{S}\left(\ldvect{c}{g}\right) & \lrdmatr{I}{g}
	\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

where \scal{m} is the vehicle mass, $\ldvect{c}{g}=\left[\ldscal{c}{g,x}, \ldscal{c}{g,y},
		\ldscal{c}{g,z}\right]^{\top}$ is the position vector of the centre of gravity relative to the
body-fixed origin, $\matr{S}\left(\cdot\right)$ denotes the skew-symmetric matrix operator, and
\lrdmatr{I}{g} is the moment of inertia matrix about the centre of gravity. The off-diagonal blocks
capture the coupling between translational and rotational dynamics. Finally the rigid-body inertia
matrix can be expressed as
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{M}{rb} = \begin{bmatrix}
		                  \scal{m}                   & 0                          & 0                          & 0                          & \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, z}  & -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, y} \\
		                  0                          & \scal{m}                   & 0                          & -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, z} & 0                          & \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, x}  \\
		                  0                          & 0                          & \scal{m}                   & \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, y}  & -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, x} & 0                          \\
		                  0                          & -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, z} & \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, y}  & \ldscal{I}{xx}             & -\ldscal{I}{xy}            & -\ldscal{I}{xz}            \\
		                  \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, z}  & 0                          & -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, x} & -\ldscal{I}{xy}            & \ldscal{I}{yy}             & -\ldscal{I}{yz}            \\
		                  -\scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, y} & \scal{m} \ldscal{c}{g, x}  & 0                          & -\ldscal{I}{xz}            & -\ldscal{I}{yz}            & \ldscal{I}{zz}
	                  \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}

with \ldscal{I}{xx}, \ldscal{I}{yy}, \ldscal{I}{zz} being the moments of inertia about the
body-fixed axes, and \ldscal{I}{xy}, \ldscal{I}{xz}, \ldscal{I}{yz} being the products of inertia.

The added-mass inertia matrix \lrdmatr{M}{a} is defined analogously as as diagonal matrix
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{M}{a} & = \diag{\left(\ldscal{M}{a, u}, \ldscal{M}{a, v}, \ldscal{M}{a, w}, \ldscal{M}{a, p}, \ldscal{M}{a, q}, \ldscal{M}{a, r}\right)}
\end{align}

where the coefficients are usually determined experimentally.

The hydrodynamic damping matrix $\matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right)$ is typically partitioned into
linear and quadratic components:
\begin{align}
	\matr{D}\left(\vect{\nu}\right) & = \lrdmatr{D}{l} + \lrdmatr{D}{q}\left(\vect{\nu}\right).
\end{align}

They are in turn represented as a diagonal matrices,
\begin{align}
	\lrdmatr{D}{l} & = \diag{\left( \ldscal{D}{l,u}, \ldscal{D}{l,v}, \ldscal{D}{l,w}, \ldscal{D}{l,p}, \ldscal{D}{l,q}, \ldscal{D}{l,r} \right)}                                           \\
	\lrdmatr{D}{q} & = \diag{\left( \ldscal{D}{q,u}\abs{u}, \ldscal{D}{q,v}\abs{v}, \ldscal{D}{q,w}\abs{w}, \ldscal{D}{q,p}\abs{p}, \ldscal{D}{q,q}\abs{q}, \ldscal{D}{q,r}\abs{r} \right)}
\end{align}

where the coefficients are also usually determined experimentally.
