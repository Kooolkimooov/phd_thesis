\section{Catenary model derivation}
\label{mb_catenary_model_derivation}

In order to derive the shape of a homogeneous, infinitely flexible, non elastic cable only
subjected to its own weight which is uniformly distributed along its length, we consider a cable of
length \catL{} and mass per unit length $\lambda_m$ hanging between two fixed points \fpoint{A}{v}
and \fpoint{B}{v} whose coordinates are expressed in the plane \lplanefullxz{v} where
\ldpoint{O}{v} is the origin of the coordinate system as well as the lowest point of the cable.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\inputtikz{figures/catenary_derivation_schematic_0}
	\caption{
		Cable of length \catL{} hanging between \fpoint{A}{v} and \fpoint{B}{v}.
	}
	\label{mb_catenary_derivation_schematic}
\end{figure}

The overall weight of the cable is
\begin{align}
	W & = \lambda_m \catL g \label{mb_cable_weight_equation}
\end{align}

At \ldpoint{O}{v}, the tension in the cable is fully horizontal because the tangent of the cable at
that point is horizontal and the cable's infinite flexibility does not allow load to be transferred
vertically.

At a given point \fldpoint{P}{v}{1} of the cable, the tension is composed of a vertical component
and a horizontal component
\begin{align}
	T_1 & = \sqrt{T_h^2 + \left(\lambda_m L_1 g\right)^2} \label{mb_cable_tension_equation}
\end{align}

where $L_1$ is the length of the cable between \ldpoint{O}{v} and \fldpoint{P}{v}{1}.

Let us define a infinitesimal length of the cable $dL$, the infinitesimal horizontal length $dx$ is
define as
\begin{align}
	dx = \cos{\left(\omega\right)}dL
\end{align}

where $\cos{\omega} = \frac{T_h}{T_1}$. Then
\begin{align}
	dx = \frac{T_h}{T_1}dL = \frac{T_h}{\sqrt{T_h^2 + \left(\lambda_m L_1 g\right)^2}}dL
\end{align}

with a change of variable $\catC = \frac{\lambda_mg}{T_h}$
\begin{align}
	dx & = \frac{\frac{\lambda_mg}{\catC}}{\sqrt{\left(\frac{\lambda_mg}{\catC}\right)^2 + \left(\lambda_m L_1 g\right)^2}}dL \\
	   & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \left(L_1\catC\right)^2}}dL
\end{align}

integrating between \ldpoint{O}{v} and \fldpoint{P}{v}{1} gives
\begin{align}
	x & = \int_0^{L_1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \left(L_1\catC\right)^2}}dL \\
	  & = \frac{\sinh^{-1}{\left(L_1\catC\right)}}{\catC}
\end{align}

Then, finally, let us define the vertical infinitesimal length $dz$
\begin{align}
	dz = \tan{\left(\omega\right)}dx
\end{align}

where $\tan{\omega} = \frac{W_1}{T_h}$ with $W_1$ being the weight of the cable between \ldpoint{O}{v} and \fldpoint{p}{v}{1}. Then
\begin{align}
	dz & = \frac{W}{T_h}dx = \frac{\lambda_m L_1 g}{T_h}dx \\
	   & = L_1\catC dx                                     \\
	   & = \sinh{\left(x~\catC\right)} dx
\end{align}

integrating between \ldpoint{O}{v} and \fldpoint{P}{v}{1} gives
\begin{align}
	z & = \int_0^{L_1} \sinh{\left(x~\catC\right)} dx \\
	  & = \frac{\cosh{(x~\catC)} - 1}{\catC}
\end{align}
