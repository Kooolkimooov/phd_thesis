\section{Augmented catenary model experimental validation}
\label{ex_catenary}

The motivation and objective of these experiments was to study the accuracy of catenary models in
estimating the shape of tethers in water under dynamic conditions. This model is needed for model
based control of tethered underwater vehicles, for example obstacle avoidance.

\subsection{Description of the experiment}

The experiment takes place in a 7.2 m long, 4.2 m wide and 3 m deep tank
(Figure~\ref{ex_catenary_robot_in_tank}). The cable's and robot's positions are recorded by a
5-camera, \qty{100}{\Hz} \emph{Qualisys}\footnote{\url{www.qualisys.com/cameras/underwater}}
underwater motion capture system whose calibration gives a standard deviation of \qty{2.2}{\mm}
across the tank's volume for a known object's length. The robot as well as each cable are equipped
with passive reflective markers (\cref{ex_catenary_robot_markers,ex_catenary_all_cables}) and the
cable's markers are evenly spaced out by \qty{20}{\centi\meter}. The cables are attached to the
robot and to a fixed point on side of the tank with thin wire
(\cref{ex_catenary_robot_cable_attachment_point,ex_catenary_rod_cable_attachment_point}) so that
the joint between the tether and its attachment point is as close to a theoretical spherical joint
as possible. The cables used are shown in \cref{ex_catenary_all_cables} and their characteristics
are in \cref{ex_catenary_cables_characteristics}.

The experiment is repeated three times for each cable, under six experimental conditions, which
makes it possible to test the reactions of more or less taut cables with accelerations of different
amplitudes. Each experimental condition is a combination of a direction of motion (sway or surge),
a speed (\qty{0.3}{\m\per\s} or \qty{0.6}{\m\per\s}) and a starting point characterized by its
horizontal distance from the fixation point (about \qty{1.5}{\m} or \qty{2}{\m}).

In every experiment the robot receives the following commands:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item stabilisation with auto-depth at \qty{1}{\m} depth for \qty{2}{\s},
	\item abrupt start with constant surge or sway command applied for \qty{2.5}{\s},
	\item reversing of the thrusters for \qty{3}{\s} with a doubled velocity of opposite direction, to obtain
	      an abrupt stop,
	\item slow down the thruster for half the initial velocity during \qty{0.2}{\s} and,
	\item deactivation of the auto-depth control and complete stop of the thrusters after \qty{13}{\s}. The
	      robot then drifts slowly.
\end{enumerate}
