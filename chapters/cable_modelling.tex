\section{Introduction}

This chapter focuses on the modeling of a cable --particularly its shape-- attached to underwater
robots under different usage modes. With an accurate model, the cable can be usued as a
localization method for the robots as well as a basis for control constraints. Its usage in the
control scheme of the system constrains the model to be as computationally efficient as possible
while staying accurate. The model must also be inferred from available sensors: IMUs, water
pressure sensor and cameras.

\section{Catenary model}

\subsection{Definition}

The shape of a homogeneous hanging cable between two points, with fixed length \catL, only
subjected to its weight, is defined by a catenary curve (\cref{cm_catenary_curve}). Underwater
cable are also subjected to buoyancy which is opposite to weight in direction; the resultant of
those two forces must be non-zeros for the catenary model to apply.

The catenary curve lies in a vertical plane \lplanefullxz{v}, associated to the Cartesian frame
\lframefull{v} with \ldpoint{O}{v} being the lowest point of the curve and \ldvect{z}{v} being the
axis along which gravity exerts its effet in this frame. Curve points coordinates $\fpoint{P}{v} =
	\left(\coord{x}{v},~0,~\coord{z}{v}\right) \in \lframe{v}$ between two attachment points
$\fpoint{A}{v}=\left(-\catD-\catdD,~0,~\catH+\catdH\right)$ and
$\fpoint{B}{v}=\left(\catD,~0,~\catdH\right)$ are given by:
\begin{align}
	\coord{z}{v} = \frac{\cosh{(\coord{x}{v}~\catC)} - 1}{C},~\coord{x}{v}\in\left[-\catD-\catdD,~\catD\right]
	\label{cm_catenary_equation}
\end{align}
where $\catC \in \mathbb{R}^*_+$ is the catenary parameter.

\subsection{Parameter derivation}

The construction parameters \catD{}, \catdD{}, \catH{} and \catdH{}, the cable length \catL{} as
well as its mass are closely related to the overall catenary shape parameter \catC{}.

\subsubsection{From mass and tension}

By construction (\cref{mb_catenary_model_derivation}), the catenary parameter \catC{} can be
determined by
\begin{align}
	\catC = \frac{\lambda_mg}{T_h}
	\label{cm_catenary_parameter_mt}
\end{align}

with $g$ the gravitational acceleration, $\lambda_c$ the cable linear mass and $T_h$ the horizontal
component of the tension in the cable. The horizontal component of the tension is constant
throughout the cable.

Knowing the linear volume $\lambda_v$ of the cable, the buoyancy of the cable can be used to extend
\cref{cm_catenary_parameter_mt}
\begin{align}
	\catC = \frac{\left(\lambda_m - \lambda_v\rho_w\right)g}{T_h}
\end{align}

with $\rho_w$ the water density.

\subsubsection{From endpoints coordinates}

Let $L$ be the known cable length, $2\catdD+\catD$ the horizontal distance and \catdH{} the
difference in elevation between the cable end points \fpoint{A}{v} and \fpoint{B}{v}. The catenary
parameter $C$ is estimated by finding the root of function $f$ which relates the curve's length and
\catC{} (\cite{laranjeiraCatenarybasedVisualServoing2020}) (using Brent's
method~\cite{brentAlgorithmGuaranteedConvergence1971}):
\begin{align*}
	f(C)=C^2 \left(L^2 - \Delta H^2\right) - 4 \sinh^2(lC/2)
\end{align*}
