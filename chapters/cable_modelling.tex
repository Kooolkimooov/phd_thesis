\section{Introduction}

This chapter focuses on the modeling of a cable --particularly its shape-- attached to underwater
robots under different usage modes. With an accurate model, the cable can be usued as a
localization method for the robots as well as a basis for control constraints. Its usage in the
control scheme of the system constrains the model to be as computationally efficient as possible
while staying accurate. The model must also be inferred from available sensors: IMUs, water
pressure sensor and cameras.

\section{Catenary model}

\subsection{Definition}

The catenary model describes the static equilibrium shape of a homogeneous, flexible cable
suspended between two fixed points under the influence of gravity. This shape, depicted in
\cref{cm_catenary_curve}, is defined when the cable has a fixed length $\catL$ and is solely
subjected to its own weight. In underwater environments, the cable also experiences a buoyant
force, which acts in the opposite direction to weight. For the catenary model to remain valid, the
net vertical force—resulting from the combination of weight and buoyancy—must be non-zero.

The resulting curve lies within a vertical plane, denoted by \lplanefullxz{v}, and is described
relative to a Cartesian reference frame \lframefull{v}. The origin \ldpoint{O}{v} is set at the
lowest point of the curve, and the axis \ldvect{z}{v} is aligned with the direction of
gravitational action in this frame. The coordinates of the points along the curve are expressed as
$\fpoint{P}{v} = \left(\coord{x}{v},~0,~\coord{z}{v}\right) \in \lframe{v}$, bounded by the
attachment points $\fpoint{A}{v}=\left(-\catD+\catdD,~0,~\catH+\catdH\right)$ and
$\fpoint{B}{v}=\left(\catD,~0,~\catdH\right)$. The elevation $\coord{z}{v}$ of each point is given
by the standard catenary equation:

\begin{align}
	\coord{z}{v} = \frac{\cosh{(\coord{x}{v}~\catC)} - 1}{\catC}, \quad \coord{x}{v} \in \left[-\catD+\catdD,~\catD\right]
	\label{cm_catenary_equation}
\end{align}

Here, $\catC \in \mathbb{R}^*_+$ is referred to as the catenary parameter, which governs the
curvature of the cable.

\subsection{Parameters derivation}

The construction parameters \catD{}, \catdD{}, \catH{} and \catdH{} as well as the cable length
\catL{} are closely related to the overall catenary shape parameter \catC{}
\begin{align}
	C              & = \frac{4\catH+2\catdH+4\catL\sqrt{\frac{\catH\left(\catH+\catdH\right)}{\catL^2-\catdH^2}}}{\catL^2-\left(2\catH+\catdH\right)^2} \\
	\catH          & = \frac{\cosh{(\catD\catC)} - 1}{C}                                                                                                \\
	\catH + \catdH & = \frac{\cosh{((\catD+\catdD)\catC)} - 1}{C}
\end{align}

In practice, all of these parameters are not all available, however there are other ways to
determine the catenary parameter.

\subsubsection{From mass and tension}

By construction (\cref{mb_catenary_model_derivation}), the catenary parameter \catC{} can be
determined by
\begin{align}
	\catC = \frac{\lambda_mg}{T_h}
	\label{cm_catenary_parameter_mt}
\end{align}

with $g$ the gravitational acceleration, $\lambda_c$ the cable linear mass and $T_h$ the horizontal
component of the tension in the cable. The horizontal component of the tension is constant
throughout the cable.

Knowing the linear volume $\lambda_v$, the buoyancy of the cable can be used to extend
\cref{cm_catenary_parameter_mt}
\begin{align}
	\catC = \frac{\left(\lambda_m - \lambda_v\rho_w\right)g}{T_h}
\end{align}

with $\rho_w$ the water density.

\subsubsection{From endpoints coordinates}

Knowing the cable end points \fpoint{A}{v} and \fpoint{B}{v}, \catdH{} and $l$ can be determined
(\cref{cm_catenary_curve}). If the cable length \catL{} is also known, the catenary parameter
\catC{} is estimated by finding the root of function which relates the curve's length and \catC{}
(\cite{laranjeiraCatenarybasedVisualServoing2020}) (using Brent's
method~\cite{brentAlgorithmGuaranteedConvergence1971}):
\begin{align}
	\catC^2 \left(\catL^2 - \catdH^2\right) - 4 \sinh^2(l\catC/2) = 0
\end{align}

\subsubsection{From endpoint coordinate and tangent}

As described in~\cite{druptInertialmeasurementbasedCatenaryShape2022}, the catenary parameter can
be determined Knowing the coordinates of one of the enpoints as well as the tangent vector at a
known point $s$ along the length of the cable. The catenary parameter is then
\begin{align}
	\catC = \frac{2\abs{\tan{\left(\beta\right)}}}{\catL - \catdH - 2s}
\end{align}

with $\beta$ the angle between the tangent vector and the horizontal. Note that this method is
valid only if $s$ is very small and $\catL >> \catdH$.
