\section{Introduction}
\label{cable_modelling_introduction}

This chapter focuses on the modeling of a cable --particularly its shape-- attached to underwater
robots under different usage modes. With an accurate model, the cable can be usued as a
localization method for the robots as well as a basis for control constraints. Its usage in the
control scheme of the system constrains the model to be as computationally efficient as possible
while staying accurate. The model must also be inferred from available sensors: IMUs, water
pressure sensor and cameras.

The contributions of this chapter are the following:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \Cref{cable_modelling_model}: The introduction of an augmented catenary model that improves the accuracy of the model;
\end{itemize}

\section{Modeling and estimation}
\label{cable_modelling_model}

\subsection{Standard catenary model}
\label{cable_modelling_catenary_model}

The shape of a homogeneous hanging cable, with fixed length \catL, only subjected to its weight and
buoyancy, is defined by a catenary curve (\cref{cable_modelling_catenary_curve}). Underwater cable
are also subjected to buoyancy which is opposite to weight; the resultant of those two force must
be non-zeros for the catenary model to apply.

The catenary model lies in a vertical plane \lplanefullxz{v}, associated to the Cartesian frame
\lframefull{v} with \ldpoint{O}{v} being the lowest point of the curve and \ldvect{z}{v} being
colinear to \ldvect{z}{w}. Curve points coordinates $\fpointfull{P}{v} \in \lframe{v}$ between two
attachment points \fpoint{A}{v} and \fpoint{B}{v} are given by:
\begin{align}
	\fpoint{P}{v} = \left[\coord{x}{v},~0,~\frac{\cosh{(\coord{x}{v}~\catC)} - 1}{C}\right]^T,~\coord{x}{v}\in\left[-\catD-\catdD,~\catD\right]
	\label{cable_modelling_catenary_equation}
\end{align}
where $\catC \in \mathbb{R}^*_+$ is the catenary parameter.

\subsection{Augmented catenary model}
\label{cable_modelling_augmented_catenary_model}

In dynamical regimes, the cable is subjected to additional hydrodynamic forces---primarily drag and
added mass effects. Drag force is proportional to the square of the relative fluid velocity and
depends on the cable's orientation, with drag coefficients varying significantly between normal
($\dragCt \approx \num{1}$) and tangential ($\dragCt \approx \num{0.02}$)
components~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011, o.aFiniteElementCablemodel2018}. Added mass effects
generate forces proportional to the cable's acceleration, further complicating the dynamics. These
hydrodynamic forces cause the cable to deviate from the static catenary shape in two primary ways:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Forces normal to the catenary plane \lplane{v} cause out-of-plane deformations;
	\item Forces inside the catenary plane cause in-plane deformations.
\end{itemize}

At moderate Reynolds numbers ($\Reynolds \approx \numrange{1e3}{1e5}$), typical for underwater
vehicle operations, these hydrodynamic forces can significantly alter the cable shape even at
relatively low speeds. The resulting shape depends on the balance between gravitational/buoyancy
forces and these velocity-dependent hydrodynamic effects.

In the steady state case where all points on the cable move at the same velocity, they are
subjected to the same drag forces which is equivalent to adding a component to the gravitational
acceleration. As a consequence, the resulting uniform acceleration of the cable is tilted, and its
norm is increased depending on the velocity. The addition of two degrees of freedom in the cable
shape model to account for this effect is used here to extend the standard catenary model defined
in \cref{cable_modelling_catenary_model}.

The first degree of freedom is a rotation of angle $\gamma$ around the catenary axis \vect{e},
which is defined as the unit vector that connects cable
endpoints~\cite{druptInertialmeasurementbasedCatenaryShape2022}
(\cref{cable_modelling_gamma_rotation}). This is to consider the deformations of a tether subjected
to sway motions, \emph{i.e.} the cable ends move out of the vertical plane, namely \lplane{v}. This
rotation applied to \lframe{v} gives \lframefull{\gamma}, respectively.

	\centering

	}

The second degree of freedom, a rotation of angle $\theta$ in \lplane{\gamma}
(\cref{cable_modelling_theta_rotation}), considers tether deformations caused by surge motions,
\textit{i.e.} the two cable ends get closer or further away. The approximation of uniform
hydrodynamic forces along the cable results in a uniform tilted acceleration
\ldvect{a}{\theta\gamma} with respect to gravity. Let \lframefull{\theta\gamma} be the associated
Cartesian frame where \ldvect{z}{\theta\gamma} is parallel to \ldvect{a}{\theta\gamma} and
\ldvect{y}{\theta\gamma} is parallel to \ldvect{y}{\gamma}. The two ends of the cable can be
expressed in \lframe{\theta\gamma} and~\cref{cable_modelling_catenary_equation} is used to
construct the oriented catenary. This 2-DOF model is named the \emph{\thetagammamodel{} model}.

\begin{figure}
	\centering
	\inputtikz{figures/tilted_catenary}
	\caption{
		\gammamodel{} (red) and \thetagammamodel{} (green) catenary for $\theta = \qty{30}{\degree}$
	}
	\label{cable_modelling_theta_rotation}
\end{figure}

Note that zero values for $\gamma$ and $\theta$ yield the \emph{standard catenary} model. If
$\theta$ only is set to zero, the model presented
in~\cite{druptInertialmeasurementbasedCatenaryShape2022} can be obtained, and is referenced as
\emph{\gammamodel{}}.

\subsection{Curvilinear discretization and residual}
\label{cable_modelling_discretization}

Let us consider $n+1$ 3D measurement points distributed along the cable's length. Accordingly, the
model is discretized with respect to the curvilinear abscissa.

Henceforth, the symbols $m,v, \gamma$ and $\theta\gamma$ are used as indexes referring to the
measurements and the estimates of the \textit{standard}, \gammamodel{} and \thetagammamodel{}
catenary model variations, respectively. Model points are written:
\begin{align*}
	\fllvect{P}{w}{\ast}{k}=({}^{w}X_{k}^{\ast}, {}^{w}Y_{k}^{\ast}, {}^{w}Z_{k}^{\ast}) \\
	k\inzeroton, \ast\in \left\{ m, v, \gamma, \theta\gamma \right\}
\end{align*}
where indexes $k=0$ and $k=n$ are the tether endpoints, also indexed $i$ and $f$.

Model variants accuracies are computed by means of the residuals:
\begin{align}
	\res{\ast} & = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\norm{ \fllvect{P}{w}{m}{k} - \fllvect{P}{w}{\ast}{k} }}, ~
	\ast\in \left\{v, \gamma, \theta\gamma \right\}
	\label{cable_modelling_residual}
\end{align}

\subsection{Model parameters estimation}
\label{cable_modelling_estimation}

\subsubsection{Catenary parameter}

Let $L$ be the known tether length, $l$ the horizontal distance and $\Delta H$ the difference in
elevation between the tether attachment points calculated from the measurements of the initial and
final attachment points \fllvect{P}{\Diamond}{m}{i} and \fllvect{P}{\Diamond}{m}{f}, with
$\Diamond\in\{v, \gamma, \theta\gamma\}$ the frame in which the points are expressed
(\cref{cable_modelling_catenary_curve}). The catenary parameter $C$ is estimated by finding the
root of function $f$ which relates the curve's length
and~$C$~\cite{laranjeiraCatenarybasedVisualServoing2020} (using Brent's
method~\cite{brentAlgorithmGuaranteedConvergence1971}):
\begin{align*}
	f(C)=C^2 \left(L^2 - \Delta H^2\right) - 4 \sinh^2(lC/2)
\end{align*}

\subsubsection{Augmented model parameters}

Let $\res{\theta\gamma}\left(\theta, \gamma\right)$ be the accuracy written as a function of
$\theta$ and $\gamma$~\cref{cable_modelling_residual}. Angles $\theta$ and $\gamma$ are estimated
by minimizing this function by means of the trust region reflective
algorithm~\cite{branchSubspaceInteriorConjugate1999}:
\begin{align*}
	(\theta, \gamma) = \argmin_{(\theta, \gamma)\in\left[-\pi,\pi\right]^2}{\res{\theta\gamma}\left(\theta, \gamma\right)}
\end{align*}

The initial estimates $\theta_0$ and $\gamma_0$ are respectively set to zero and to the angle
between $\lplane{\gamma_{0}}$ and $\lplane{v}$, where $\lplane{\gamma_0}$ is the closest plane to
all measured points.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{cable_modelling_conclusion}

This work introduced an augmented catenary model that accounts for the hydrodynamic effects on the
tether when the ROV performs surge and sway motion. It incorporates the models presented in the
state of the art, which can be found by zeroing one or both of the angles proposed as augmentation
parameters.

The results drawn from the experiments carried out on eight different cables show that the
augmented model provides a better estimate of the shape of all cables during dynamic phases,
compared with the standard catenary model. The accuracy is best for flexible cables with adequate
negative buoyancy. In addition, cables with the lowest residuals relative to the standard catenary
model lead to the best improvements in accuracy using the augmented model. Their measured shape
features greater planarity and homogenous tilting of their support plane during motion.

In light of these results, the augmented catenary model will be used in model-based controllers to
estimate cable shape and its lowest position for deployment scenarios of tethered robots.
