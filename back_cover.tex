	\begin{abstract}

		This thesis addresses cable-aware modeling and control for chains of tethered underwater robots. An
		accurate yet computationally efficient cable model is proposed, designed to preserve real-time
		applicability while maintaining analytical tractability. Building upon the classical catenary
		formulation, a two-parameter \augmentedmodel{} is introduced to account for steady hydrodynamic
		deformations. When evaluated on a motion-capture dataset, the augmented model reduces point-cloud
		residuals by \qtyrange{10}{75}{\percent} relative to the vertical catenary for several cable
		configurations while remaining fully analytical. For control, catenary forces are embedded within a
		unified robot-chain model in which clearance, inter-robot spacing, and actuator bounds are
		formulated as geometry-consistent constraints. Three control strategies are investigated: a
		constrained \acl{mpc}, a waypoint planner combined with \acl{pid} tracking, and a lightweight
		reactive approach. Their performance is assessed through numerical simulations and a scaled-down
		experimental chain system, demonstrating the feasibility of achieving practical and safe
		coordination for tethered robot formations.

	\end{abstract}
